# Napoleon Bonaparte — Historical Language Translation (Corsican & 19th Century French)

**Edition:** Edition H (Reviewed Historical-Language Edition)  
**Linguistic Context:** Napoleon's childhood mother tongue was Corsican (*corsu*), an Italo-Romance idiom closely related to medieval Tuscan. He learned French as a second language starting at age 9 at the Collège d'Autun, speaking French with a recognizable Italian/Corsican accent throughout his life.  
**Options Provided:**  
- **Target A (Historical Intimacy):** 18th/19th-Century Corsican (reflecting his private, introspective reflection on Saint Helena).  
- **Target B (Imperial Public Register):** 19th-Century French (reflecting his formal dictations to Las Cases).

---

## 1. Dialogue Script — Target A: Corsican (*Corsu*)

### Line N-L01
- **Speaker:** Napoleon (Off-Screen)
- **English Original:** *"Does France keep my laws?"*
- **Corsican:** **"A Francia tene e mo leghje?"**
- **IPA Phonetics:** /a ˈfran.tʃa ˈtɛː.ne e mo ˈlɛ.ɟe/
- **Phonetic Guide:** *Ah FRAHN-chah TEH-neh eh moh LEH-jyeh?*
- **Literal Gloss:** The France keeps the my laws?

---

### Line N-L02
- **Speaker:** Andre (Seated, Facing Camera-Right)
- **English Original:** *"Your civil code outlives your throne. Other countries borrow from it. But a law can promise equality and still deny it to someone."*
- **Corsican:** **"U vostru codice civile supravive à u vostru tronu. D'altri paesi piglianu da ellu. Ma una leghje pò prumette l'ugualità è quantunque ricusalla à qualchissia."**
- **IPA Phonetics:** /u ˈvɔː.stru ˈkɔː.di.tʃe tʃiˈviː.le su.praˈviː.ve a u ˈvɔː.stru ˈtroː.nu. ˈdal.tri paˈeː.zi ˈpi.ʎa.nu da ˈel.lu. ma ˈuː.na ˈlɛ.ɟe pɔ pruˈmɛt.te lu.gwa.liˈta ɛ kwanˈtun.kwe ri.kuˈzal.la a kwalˈkis.sja/
- **Phonetic Guide:** *Oo VOH-stroh KOH-dee-cheh chee-VEE-leh soo-prah-VEE-veh ah oo VOH-stroh TROH-noo. DAHL-tree pah-AY-zee PEEL-yah-noo dah EL-loo. Mah OO-nah LEH-jyeh poh proo-MET-teh loo-gwah-lee-TAH eh kwahn-TOON-kweh ree-koo-ZAHL-lah ah kwahl-KEES-syah.*
- **Literal Gloss:** The your code civil outlives to the your throne. Other countries take from it. But a law can promise the-equality and nevertheless deny-it to someone.

### Line N-L03
- **Speaker:** Napoleon (Off-Screen)
- **English Original:** *"And do they call me great?"*
- **Corsican:** **"È mi chjamanu grande?"**
- **IPA Phonetics:** /ɛ mi ˈkjaː.ma.nu ˈgran.de/
- **Phonetic Guide:** *Eh mee KYAH-mah-noo GRAHN-deh?*
- **Literal Gloss:** And me do-they-call great?

---

### Line N-L04
- **Speaker:** Andre (Seated & Audio Overlap on Table Cut)
- **English Original:** *"Some do. Others ask about the wars and the power you kept for yourself. When they say greatness, they still argue about the price."*
- **Corsican:** **"Qualchì unu sì. D'altri dumandanu di e guerre è di u putere ch'è vo avete tenutu per voi stessi. Quand'elli dicenu grandezza, disputanu sempre di u prezzu."**
- **IPA Phonetics:** /kwalˈki ˈuː.nu ˈsi. ˈdal.tri duˈman.da.nu di e ˈgwɛr.re ɛ di u puˈteː.re kɛ vo aˈveː.te teˈnuː.tu per voi ˈstes.si. kwanˈdel.li ˈdiː.tʃe.nu granˈdɛt.tsa disˈpuː.ta.nu ˈsɛm.pre di u ˈprɛt.tsu/
- **Phonetic Guide:** *Kwahl-KEE OO-noo SEE. DAHL-tree doo-MAHN-dah-noo dee eh GWEHR-reh eh dee oo poo-TAY-reh keh voh ah-VAY-teh teh-NOO-too pehr voy STES-see. Kwahn-DEL-lee DEE-cheh-noo grahn-DET-tsah, dees-POO-tah-noo SEM-preh dee oo PRET-tsoo.*
- **Literal Gloss:** Some one yes. Others ask of the wars and of the power that you have kept for yourselves. When-they say greatness, they-dispute always of the price.
