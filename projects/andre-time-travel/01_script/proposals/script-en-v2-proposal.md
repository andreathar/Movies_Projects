# English Screenplay — v2 PROPOSAL

Status: **proposal for historian (T05) and Andre's review. Not canonical.** The canonical text remains `../script-en.md` + `../dialogue-lines.csv` until the producer integrates an approved version. All dialogue is fictional; factual anchors keep the source IDs from `00_brief/research/source-registry.json`. New factual claims introduced here are flagged `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

Line IDs are preserved so translations, atlas cards and CSV rows stay addressable. Word counts and ≈ seconds use 2.3 words/s as a *placeholder only*; the scratch track (P0-4) is the timing authority.

## What changes and why

| Problem in v1 (evidence) | v2 fix |
|---|---|
| Five chapters share one template; "argue" ×4 | Each chapter gets its own shape; "argue" used once, as the hinge |
| Andre's answers are uniformly confident; no arc | Fluent → slower → warm → caught off guard → cannot answer |
| Ending's turn inward is untriggered | Napoleon asks Andre about *his* world (N-L03); ending answers it |
| Napoleon duplicates Caesar | Napoleon shortened and repurposed; Caesar keeps the "republic" question |
| C-L04 states the theme | Caesar's chapter ends on Caesar's uncertainty, not Andre's thesis |
| O-L04 explains itself | Cut to twelve words |
| OUT-L01 "they" unanchored | Closes the circle on "what came next" |

Performance arc for Andre (voice direction, one word per chapter): **fluent · slower · warm · caught · silent**.

## C — Julius Caesar (Rome, early 44 BCE) — ~40 s

**C-L01 — Caesar, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Does my name survive?

**C-L02 — Andre** *(change; 22 w ≈ 9.5 s, was 30 w)*
> It survives. People still study the victories and the reforms. They also study what one man's power did to a republic.

**C-L03 — Caesar, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Then they remember a tyrant?

**C-L04 — Andre** *(change; 17 w ≈ 7.5 s)*
> They remember a question. Could the republic survive you? They are still asking it — about other men.

Eye cue A: focused confidence at "It survives." · Eye cue B: certainty slips at "survive you?" — hold through "other men" (he hears that he became a precedent).

Factual note: unchanged anchors [1]. "Still asking it about other men" is interpretation, not a claim about any named person.

## K — Genghis Khan (campaign ger, 1227) — ~42 s

**K-L01 — Khan, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Will my sons keep what I have made?

**K-L02 — Andre** *(change; 26 w ≈ 11 s)*
> They will make it larger than you can picture. Merchants, ideas, and knowledge will cross it. So will the news of cities that are no longer there.

**K-L03 — Khan, offscreen** *(keep)*
> And what will they call me?

**K-L04 — Andre** *(change; 16 w ≈ 7 s)*
> Founder. Conqueror. Both words are still fighting over you. Neither one can make the dead speak.

Eye cue A: controlled pride at "larger than you can picture." · Eye cue B: a single restrained blink at "no longer there"; stillness through "the dead speak."

Factual note: successors' expansion, Eurasian exchange and destructive conquest [2][15]. "Cities that are no longer there" stays general — no named city, no casualty figure.

## G — Galileo Galilei (Arcetri, 1634) — ~42 s

**G-L01 — Galileo, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Am I finally believed?

**G-L02 — Andre** *(change; 21 w ≈ 9 s)*
> Believed, taught — and turned into a simple story: one man against the whole world. The truth was harder, and more interesting.

**G-L03 — Galileo, offscreen** *(keep — strongest question in the film)*
> And what survives of the question?

**G-L04 — Andre** *(change; 21 w ≈ 9 s)*
> Your observations. And one lesson from your trial that people keep relearning: silencing a man does not settle a question.

Eye cue A: exhausted attention brightening at "more interesting." · Eye cue B: quiet resolve at "does not settle."

Factual note: trial and house arrest [3][12]. No "and yet it moves." "Keep relearning" is interpretation.

## N — Napoleon Bonaparte (Saint Helena, 1816) — ~32 s (shortened; the hinge)

**N-L01 — Napoleon, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Does France keep my laws?

**N-L02 — Andre** *(change; 21 w ≈ 9 s)*
> Your code outlives your throne. Other nations borrow from it. It promises equality — and still finds people to leave out.

**N-L03 — Napoleon, offscreen** *(change — the only time a figure asks about Andre's world)*
> And you. Where you come from — do they still argue about the price?

**N-L04 — Andre** *(change; 9 w ≈ 4 s; first time he is on the back foot)*
> Every day. We are still not sure who pays it.

Eye cue A: pride at "outlives your throne." · Eye cue B: not wounded pride — **attention**: Napoleon watches Andre fail to be superior. Contained downward glance after "who pays it."

Factual note: code's influence and its exclusions [4][17]. Napoleon's question is fictional and belongs to the film's argument, not to his biography.

Timing note: the 10 s removed here are proposed for Oppenheimer (+6) and the ending (+4). Producer decides via manifest/shot-list.

## O — J. Robert Oppenheimer (near Trinity base camp, 15 July 1945) — ~48 s

**O-L01 — Oppenheimer, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Does the test work?

**O-L02 — Andre** *(change; 30 w ≈ 13 s; give the names room)*
> It works. Three weeks later, Hiroshima. Three days after that, Nagasaki. The war ends that summer. What it cost — and whether it had to — people are still asking.

**O-L03 — Oppenheimer, offscreen** *(keep)*
> Can you tell me whether it was right?

**O-L04 — Andre** *(change; 12 w ≈ 5 s; then the longest silence in the film)*
> I can tell you what came next. I can't tell you that.

Eye cue A: anticipation empties at "Hiroshima." · Eye cue B: near-motionless at "I can't tell you that." Hold at least 4 s of silence before the cut.

Factual note: Trinity 16 July; Hiroshima 6 Aug (21 days = three weeks); Nagasaki 9 Aug (three days) [5][18][19]. "The war ends that summer" — surrender announced 15 Aug / signed 2 Sep 1945 **[NEEDS SOURCE — add to registry]**. "Whether it had to" references the documented debate [9] without asserting a cause of surrender.

## Visual rules that travel with this script

1. The table is Andre's. It travels with him. The ring stain and the near-corner chip appear in every era — they are the proof and the match-cut key.
2. Match cuts happen on the **insert** (hand + stain + chip, locked lens height), not on the wide.
3. The seat opposite is era-specific; the table never is.
4. Nothing on the table is readable: no brand, no stamp, manuscript out of focus.
5. One eye identity per figure; expression B is a minimal delta, not a new casting.
