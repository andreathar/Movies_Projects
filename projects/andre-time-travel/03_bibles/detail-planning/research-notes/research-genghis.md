# Genghis Khan (Chinggis) — material-culture research notes for historian sheet
Scene: private conversation in a campaign ger, Western Xia campaign, summer 1227. Camera sees him only from behind / below the neck / hands / feet. Researched 2026-10-09. Read/web only; nothing written to the repo.

## Tag legend
- **DOC** = documented about Chinggis himself (note whether first-hand or second-hand).
- **RANK** = documented for Mongol rulers/nobles of the period (named person or court other than Chinggis, or class of person).
- **GENERIC** = period-generic Mongol/steppe evidence, or later/modern evidence; not tied to rank or to him.
- **NONE** = no source found.

## Read this first: the chronology problem
No eyewitness description of Chinggis's tent, clothes or boots exists in a text I could reach. Source dates, nearest to 1227 first:
- Secret History of the Mongols (SH): composed after his death (c. 1228 or later); narrative, not an inventory. Closest in time and in rank.
- Zhao Hong, *Mengda beilu* (1221, Song envoy; he met Muqali at Yanjing, not Chinggis). The only contemporary outside observer, and what he says about Chinggis is hearsay.
- Peng Daya / Xu Ting, *Heida shilue* (c. 1233-36, Ögedei's court). Nine years after 1227.
- Carpini (1246, Güyük's enthronement and Batu's camp) and Rubruck (1253-55, Batu, Möngke at Karakorum). 19-28 years after 1227.
- Marco Polo (Kublai, 1270s-90s) and the Taipei album (14th century), Yuanshi (1370): later still.
Every Rubruck/Carpini/Polo item below is a RANK or GENERIC proxy, not evidence about 1227.

## 2. Floor and interior

### Structure and hearth
- Round lattice-and-pole tent with smoke/light hole, felt cover, felt door.
  - Carpini (Dawson): "Their dwelling-places are round like tents and are made of twigs and slender sticks. At the top in the middle there is a round opening which lets in the light, and is also to enable the smoke to escape, for they always make their fire in the middle. Both the sides and the roof are covered with felt, and the doors also are made of felt." [S2] **GENERIC/RANK (1246)**
  - Rubruck: "a circular frame of interlaced sticks converging into a little round hoop on the top, from which projects above a collar as a chimney, and this (framework) they cover over with white felt." [S1]
  - Rubruck on the door: "holding up the felt which hung before it" (at Möngke's) [S1].
- Central fire in a grate (the ruler's tent): Rubruck at Möngke's: "The house was all covered inside with cloth of gold, and there was a fire of briars and wormwood roots--which grow here to great size--and of cattle dung, in a grate in the center of the dwelling." [S1] **RANK** (1254)
- Fuel: *Heida shilue*: "其爨，草炭。〈牛馬糞。〉" (their cooking fuel: grass-charcoal, i.e. dung). [S7] **RANK/GENERIC** (c. 1235)
- SH uses "fireplace" and treats it as the tent's centre: at the Teb Tenggeri killing, his "hat fell in front of the fireplace" and Chinggis's brothers "disposed themselves around the fireplace" (§245). [S5] **DOC** (a scene in Chinggis's own tent; narrative, not a measured description)
- Fire taboo (relevant to hands/props): Carpini: "to stick a knife into a fire, or even in any way to touch fire with a knife, or to extract meat from the cauldron with a knife" are sins (the fire would be "beheaded"). [S2] **GENERIC**

### Floor
- **NONE** for the 13th-century floor material. No source found stating felt layers, carpets, or earth. Rubruck says only that felt is made "to cover their houses and coffers, and also for bedding." [S1]
- Indirect: Rubruck left "a carpet or rug" in Batu's ordu among the baggage [S1]; Juvaini says that in an anecdote of Chinggis's reign the Mongols pitched "clean tents of white felt" for honoured Muslim merchants [S9]; Möngke's tent was lined "with cloth of gold" [S1]; Met essay on Ilkhanid royal tents (Iranian court, later) says floors were covered with "Persian rugs" (search-snippet only) [S20].
- Modern ger practice (felt over earth/wood floor) is GENERIC and modern, not period evidence. [S19]

### Seating and layout
- Rubruck: "they set up the couch of the master on the north side. The side for the women is always the east side, that is to say, on the left of the house of the master, he sitting on his couch his face turned to the south. The side for the men is the west side, that is, on the right. Men coming into the house would never hang up their bows on the side of the woman." Door "turned to the south". [S1] **RANK/GENERIC** (Batu's and Möngke's realm). Men on the west/right, women east/left.
- Rubruck at Batu (1253): "He was seated on a long seat as broad as a couch, all gilded, and with three steps leading up to it... Men were seated about on his right, and ladies on his left". [S1] **RANK**
- Carpini at Batu (1246, Dawson): he "sits raised up as if on a throne with one of his wives; the others... sit lower down on a bench in the middle; as for the rest they sit beyond them on the ground, the men on the right and the women on the left." [S2] **RANK**
- *Heida shilue*: "其位置，以中為尊，右次之，左為下" (the centre is most honoured, then the right, the left is lowest). [S7] **RANK** (c. 1235)
- SH: Chinggis's night-guards' place is a no-go: "No one is to sit above the place of the nightguards." (§278) [S5] **DOC** (ordinance attributed to him).
- Chinggis's seat, second-hand: Zhao Hong (1221): "成吉思之儀衞，建大純白旗…所坐乃金裹龍頭胡床" (Chinggis's guard raises a great plain-white banner... what he sits on is a *hu-chuang* [folding seat] wrapped in gold with dragon-head ends; the "king" [Muqali] has silver in places). [S6] **DOC (second-hand)**. Xu Ting (c. 1236, Ögedei): "韃主帳中所坐胡床，如禪寺講座，亦飾以金" (the Khan's seat in the tent is like a Zen-temple lecture seat, also gold-decorated). [S7] **RANK**.
- Mixed seating at feasts: Zhao Hong: "男女雜坐，更相酬勸不禁" (men and women sit mixed together, toasting each other freely). [S6] **RANK** (Muqali's court).
- SH: people "sat cross-legged": Belgütei (c. §77) and Chinggis's mother Hö'elün confronting him over Qasar (§244). [S5] **GENERIC** (posture).

### Tables (relevant to the film's ~75 cm table)
- Rubruck describes no table in any Mongol ger. What he places in the entry is "A bench with a skin of milk, or some other drink, and with cups" [S1] (also at Batu, "A bench with cosmos and big cups of gold and silver, ornamented with precious stones, was in the entry of the tent" [S1]). **RANK**
- Carpini at Batu: "In the middle near the door of the tent is set a table on which drinks are placed in gold and silver vessels." [S2] **RANK** (Batu, 1246). So a drinks table near the door has one witness. Height and form not given.
- Carpini on funerals: a chief "is buried with one of his dwellings, sitting in the middle of it, and they place a table in front of him, and a dish filled with meat and a goblet of mare's milk." [S2] **GENERIC** (burial ritual).
- Carpini on dining: "They do not use table-cloths or napkins." [S2]
- A standing dining table (~75 cm) with chairs/seating at it: **NONE**. The film's table should be recorded as a **deliberate narrative exception** (low drinks table or bench-with-cups is the best-supported form).
- SH "wine table" appears only as a footnote gloss for the banquet (§§213, 245, 254) [S5]; do not cite it as furniture evidence.

### Evaluation of the existing film note ("armour dropped for table scenes, light monochrome robe + dark outer coat")
- **Armour dropped**: plausible, nothing contradicts it. But no source places a ruler in armour or out of it inside a ger; Möngke is in a skin garment [S1]. Mark as inference.
- **Light robe**: partly supported. Rubruck says a lighter gown is worn when sitting in the dwelling; summer wear is silk/cotton [S1]. A white robe appears in later imagery (NPM, Kublai) and the Yuanshi "white fur / white warm cap" pairing, both later [S12, S8b]. White as a ruler's colour is supported second-hand by Zhao Hong (Chinggis's plain white banner) [S6] and by Juvaini's white felt tents for honoured guests [S9]. **Not documented for Chinggis's own body clothes.**
- **Monochrome**: Rubruck (1254) says feast outfits were "all of one color from their boots to their turbans" [S1]. That is a feast uniform system (the later *jisün*). Plausible; not documented for 1227.
- **Dark outer coat**: no source for a dark colour. Furs outside were wolf, fox, or lynx in winter [S1]. Summer: no outer coat required at lowland; highland nights possible. Mark as undocumented.
- **Add**: an open-back fur or gown with a tail exists (Carpini) if the film wants a back detail; waist pleats/cord (Xu Ting) are the best-sourced waist detail; no horse-hoof cuffs.

## 7. Gaps and uncertainties (biggest first)

1. **Genghis-specific material culture is almost entirely missing.** Only the hairstyle (second-hand), the gold-wrapped folding seat (second-hand), his white banner, the golden belt idiom, injuries, and age have DOC-level anchors. Footwear, floor, table, lamp, hands, and colour of dress are RANK/GENERIC.
2. **Footwear toe shape:** nothing earlier than the 17th century for the upturned toe (tertiary Wikipedia claim, uncited). No dated 13th-century boot found in a museum (Met, NPM, British Museum searches returned nothing relevant). Mingachevir boots mentioned but not described in detail. Khorig finds unverified.
3. **Floor material** in a 13th-century ger: not found. All floor claims are inference from modern practice or Ilkhanid-court sources.
4. **The ~75 cm table:** no standing dining table anywhere; only a drinks table near the door (Carpini at Batu) and a bench with cups (Rubruck). Record as a narrative exception.
5. **Thumb ring and calluses:** no Chinggis evidence; generic Mongol thumb draw with bone/horn rings is well supported as a practice (12th-14th c.), jade is not.
6. **Scalp hair colour and the Taipei hat:** the NPM page describes only Kublai's cap and braids. The Genghis-leaf costume description comes from a popular article (storm.mg) and the Commons file page has none. Look at the NPM digital archive entry (中-畫-000324) and the catalogue *Dschingis Khan und seine Erben* (2005) before relying on it. Face and beard descriptions (Juzjani, Zhao Hong) conflict (scanty white vs. long beard).
7. **Horse-hoof cuffs** are Manchu/Qing (V&A); not for 1227.
8. **Translation hazards:** Carpini's closure sentence is internally odd (left one tie / right three); Kerr/Hakluyt paraphrase says "hats" where Dawson says "hoods/caps"; Rubruck's text on the Silk Road site mixes Rockhill with Jackson 1990 variants in "[J: ...]" brackets; Heida shilue's 被髮椎髻 is hard to translate.
9. **Not reached:** Li Zhichang's *Xiyouji* (audience with Chinggis 1222, would be the best contemporary eyewitness of his camp), Rashid al-Din (white forelock, unverified), Juzjani in Raverty (only via a secondary article), the full Shea OA book (bot-blocked), Met "Mongolian Tent in the Ilkhanid Period" essay (snippet only), Cambridge JRAS article on felt and tent carts in the SH (not read).
10. **Setting check (outside brief):** Yuanshi, Taizu *benji*, 1227: "閏月，避暑六盤山…帝次清水縣西江…秋七月壬午，不豫。己丑，崩于薩里川哈老徒之行宮" (intercalary month: summer retreat at Liupan; camped west of the Qingshui River; 7th month renwu fell ill; jichou died at the travelling palace at Saliquan Haladu). [S8] SH §265 says instead that he "spent the summer on Časutu Mountain" (Snowy) and gives no year for it. Identification of Časutu with Liupan is not verified. Death date 18 or 25 August is uncertain. [S22]
