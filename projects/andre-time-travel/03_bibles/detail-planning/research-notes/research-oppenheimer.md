# Oppenheimer material-culture research (Trinity, night 15-16 July 1945)

Method: WebSearch + WebFetch (fetch answers came from a summarising model; quotes are as returned, short and checked against page context where possible). The Conant notes PDF was also text-extracted locally (pdftotext), so those quotes are first-hand verified. Status values: fetched-verified / search-snippet-only / could not verify.

Claim tags: DOC = documented about Oppenheimer himself. RANK/ROLE = documented for the group/role/place, not him personally. GENERIC = period/type plausible only. NONE = not found.
Certainty grade: A = primary or near-primary text/photo caption, fetched. B = reliable secondary, fetched. C = secondary/film-costume/hearsay or snippet-only. Not found = no grade.

Critical date rule: all clothing/photo evidence below is for OTHER dates (Berkeley, 1943 arrival, Sept 1945). Nothing located describes what he wore on the evening of 15 July 1945.

## 2. Floor and room

Where was he that night (documented):
- Evening: Bush, Groves and Conant arrived at Base Camp (10 miles from the bomb) about 8 pm. "After dinner at the mess and some brief explanation by Oppenheimer, Tolman, Kistiakowsky, and Rabbi in very informal conversation we went to bed." (Conant notes) - RANK/ROLE-group, DOC for Oppenheimer's presence. Grade A (S3, fetched-verified, text extracted locally). The document is the Bush-Conant Papers "Notes on the 'Trinity' Test"; internal text ("V. Bush and JBC") shows Conant wrote it; the PDF metadata author "Dick Groves" is just the uploader/file label.
- Late night: "late at night in the base camp mess hall, Oppenheimer sipped coffee, rolled smokes" and "read French poet Charles Baudelaire" - LANL paraphrase of Bird & Sherwin, American Prometheus. DOC, Grade B (S14; I could not open the book itself to confirm the original wording/page; marked could-not-verify for the primary).
- From about 1 am: Groves "stayed with the Director" for hours; Oppenheimer was at the S-10000 control shelter ("control shelter located 10,000 yards south of the point of explosion"), and "walk with him in the rain" (Farrell). DOC, Grade A (S15, S16). Groves: "After about an hours sleep I got up at 0100 and from that time on until five I was with Dr. Oppenheimer constantly." (S16)
- Bush/Conant slept in a tent: "preventing sleep in our tent" (Conant), and "The canvas slapped constantly in the high wind" (S2). RANK/ROLE.

Base Camp buildings (July 1945):
- "constructed mostly of temporary prefabricated buildings taken from Civilian Conservation Corps camps"; "It had a mess hall, barracks, shower facilities, and laboratories." RANK/ROLE, Grade B (S34, WSMR Museum part 4).
- IWM 1945 film record (May 1945 footage): "scattered wooden buildings, parked trucks"; "wooden buildings. Walls protected by banked earth"; "wind pump and water tanks." Grade A for what it describes; floors/roofs not described (S29).
- Jennet Conant (AHF ranger tour): "First, they lived in tents, then in little huts." "really primitive." Grade B, RANK/ROLE (S31).
- Capacity: designed for 160, eventually close to 300; peak about 325 (S5, S6).
- Mess hall: "Soldiers and scientists lined up at the mess hall" (photo caption); mess hall crew belonged to the 45-man Provisional Engineer Detachment (S5, S5b). Floor material of mess hall/barracks: NONE found (a generic wartime "all wood, sectional" barracks manual surfaced in search but is not Trinity-specific; treat as GENERIC only).
- Base-camp tables/furniture: NONE specific. Only the ranch-house assembly room had "Work benches and tables" (S4).

McDonald ranch house (note: the plutonium-assembly house is the Schmidt/George McDonald house about 2 miles from ground zero, NOT the Base Camp at the Dave McDonald ranch 10 miles away):
- Floors: "Floors were of wood" (Rosemary Hall, granddaughter of owners; describes the pre-army house); "adobe with pebble dash outside walls"; "inside walls were smooth plaster"; roof "tin with 18-inch anchor bolts into the adobe walls." Tag: RANK/ROLE (building), Grade B/C (family recollection of pre-1945 state) (S7).
- 1945 use: "The house stood empty until the Manhattan Project support personnel arrived in early 1945"; northeast room (master bedroom) was the assembly room, windows covered in plastic, "Work benches and tables were installed." (S4). It was the 12-13 July assembly site. No source puts Oppenheimer in it on the night of 15 July. Stone floor / plank floor: NONE found beyond "wood".
- S-10000: "concrete shelters protected by wood structures and earthen berms"; dismantled 1965 leaving a slab (S26). Interior layout/lighting: NONE. Compania Hill: only that Teller watched from there, 20 miles NW (S26); no building.

Which building plausibly hosted a private evening talk: documented venues are (a) the Base Camp mess hall (dinner; later Oppenheimer reading/coffee, per S14), and (b) the tents (Conant notes: Rabi came into the Bush/Conant tent at 3:15). No source describes any private room, office or barracks conversation. A private room scene at Base Camp is therefore invention; the mess hall (late-night, mostly empty) is the best-documented plausible location.

## 3. Hair as seen from behind

- Hempelmann (AHF oral history, interview date not on page): "he used to wear his hair very long and very curly, just like Frank." "By the time Robert came to Los Alamos, I almost did not recognize him again, because by this time, he had his hair very closely clipped." DOC, Grade B (S19). Best evidence for a short nape/ears-exposed cut at Los Alamos.
- Same interview, pre-war Berkeley: "Robert always had that porkpie hat on... I saw him in a meeting one time without his hat. It was the first time I had ever seen him without a hat." DOC (S19).
- Hat: brown porkpie, size 6 7/8 per NYT via LANL (already in film notes; S21). Sept 1945 photos captioned "broad-brimmed hat" (Wikipedia, S11), "white hat" (LIFE, S12), "light colored hat" (Commons, S10); in the grayscale Sept photo it reads as a light-toned, medium-brim hat. The brown porkpie versus light hat discrepancy is unresolved; the brown colour comes from LANL text, not from a 1945 colour source.
- Grey/greying hair, thickness, nape length in millimetres, ears: NONE. Hair on the night of 15 July: NONE.
- Colgate (AHF): "Of course the porkpie, there was just no question that this was Oppenheimer." Only the hat is described (S20).

## 4. Collar / sleeves / cuffs / belt / trousers

- "Oppenheimer wore jeans or khaki pants and a blue work shirt to work in Los Alamos" - DOC, Grade A/B (NPS, S1). That is the only primary-ish clothing statement. The blue shirt is also the film designer's emphasis (S23).
- Collar open/closed, tie at Los Alamos work: NONE. Rolled sleeves, cuff style, buttoned cuffs: NONE.
- Sept 1945 photo (own visual of S10): the figure in the light hat appears in a dark suit with jacket, light shirt and tie, with a badge at the lapel. This is a visit with press, not 15 July. Grade C (my observation).
- Jacket (tweed/khaki), corduroy trousers: NONE. Hempelmann's "ordinary suit" refers to a costume party (S19), not evidence.
- Belt: silver-and-turquoise Navajo-style buckle is a claim from film costume designer Ellen Mirojnick: "there really was a belt buckle... we recreated it" (via Below the Line, repeated in S24) and "we finally did see an image although it was faint" (S23, fetched). No photograph, archive, date, or memoirist is cited. Tag: NONE for the real object (only film-research hearsay), Grade C. Match-striking-on-buckle detail appeared only in a search snippet, not in the fetched interview: could not verify.
- Weight: NPS says 115 lb during Project Y (already in notes); Wikipedia says Army doctors considered him underweight at 128 lb (S11). Different dates; treat as a range of lean build.

## 5. Hands

- Smoking volume: "chain smoked four to five packs of cigarettes a day along with a pipe and had a persistent cough" (NPS, S1). DOC, Grade A/B.
- Berkeley student (unnamed, pre-war): "the most distinctive feature of his lectures [was] his chain smoking... puffed equally rapidly" and "he extinguished it and lit another almost in a single motion" (LANL, S21). DOC, Grade B, pre-war.
- Night of 15-16 July: "rolled smokes" in the mess hall (S14, via Bird & Sherwin). DOC, Grade B. Suggests hand-rolled cigarettes that night; the primary text was not seen.
- Brand: "Chesterfield" appears only via prop master Guillaume Delouche (Looper citing Washington Post, "only brand that Oppenheimer would smoke", "150 cigarettes every day") - film-prop hearsay, Grade C. Treat as unverified. A forum claim that he smoked a straight billiard pipe is not usable.
- Pipe: photo captions and NPS confirm a pipe exists (S1, S11). Shape/colour: NONE.
- Nervous gestures: Farrell, at zero: "He scarcely breathed. He held on to a post to steady himself." (S15, fetched). DOC, Grade A. Groves "walk with him in the rain" to calm him. "Constant fidgeting" (Dyson) came only from a search snippet (Science History Institute) - could not verify.
- Gestures with cigarettes while telling stories at parties (LANL snippet) - snippet-only.
- Thin hands/long fingers, nails, ring, notebooks, pencil, slide rule, ashtray on the night: NONE found. (Wedding ring: NONE.) Anything shown here is GENERIC invention.
- Reading object: a book of Baudelaire (S14; a Met Opera guide in search results also says he had a Baudelaire book in his pocket - snippet-only).

## 6. Plausible light sources, weather and documented night events

Weather timeline (documented):
- Conant: "From about 10:30 to 1 a.m., it blew very hard thus preventing sleep in our tent... Then it poured for about an hour!" At 3:15 "the rain having just ceased" Rabi came to the tent; coffee about 4 am; "the sky was still overcast." Grade A (S3).
- Groves: "thunder storms with lightning flashes all over the area"; "By 0400 the rain had stopped but the sky was heavily overcast." (S15/S16). Segrè: "On the evening of that day a tremendous thundershower fell on the desert" and "In the late evening we went to sleep" (S18). Bainbridge text (via S18) says the site had "none of the lightning reported by those at the Base Camp about 16,000 yards away" - i.e., lightning was seen at Base Camp. Hornig on the tower: "By then there was a violent thunder and lightning storm." (S9). Wikipedia: delay due to "rain and lightning from early that morning" (S8). Test moved from 4:00 to 5:30.
- Discrepancy: Conant (Base Camp) has the downpour roughly 1-2 am and rain ceasing about 3:15; others say rain stopped at 4:00. Exact lightning times at Base Camp: NONE.

Lighting at Base Camp:
- Generators: engineer detachment handled "generators for power"; Carl Rudder "ran the generators, pumps, wells, and power system" ("East Jesus and Socorro Light and Water Company") (S5, S28). Wattage/bulb type/layout: NONE. Whether mess hall had bare bulbs: NONE found, but electric supply from generators is documented, so electric light is plausible (RANK/ROLE, GENERIC for fixture type).
- Wind-generator charging batteries is documented for the pre-war McDonald ranch house ("tower for a wind generator the McDonalds installed to charge batteries", S4, S6); Hall says "no electricity or plumbing" at the time she describes (S7). Not Base Camp 1945.
- Lanterns / flashlights: NONE specific; GENERIC. Tower lights "to direct the B-29s" (S2) and a "string of lights on the ground" aiming point (Groves via S18) are on the range, not in a room.
- Window light: the ranch-house windows were covered in plastic (S4); no window detail for mess hall.
- Ambient sound/atmosphere documented: wind, canvas slapping (S2, S3); frogs in a pond by the camp "all night long" (Frank Oppenheimer, WBUR, S27); radio kept on (Elsie McMillan, in Los Alamos, S36 - not at the site).
- Other documented items: bets (Oppenheimer's $10 against Kistiakowsky's month's pay, S17), Rabi's pool of 18,000 tons (S3); Oppenheimer and Groves "go outside and discuss the weather every five or ten minutes" (S2). "Pacing" and "reading in his room": pacing NONE found; Baudelaire reading documented only as mess hall (S14).

## Gaps / uncertainties (summary)

1. Footwear on 15 July 1945: NONE. Only "cowboy boots" (1943, one eyewitness, snippet-verified) and Sept 1945 white overshoes (Wikipedia caption + own low-res visual; no archive caption confirmed).
2. Collar, sleeves, cuffs, jacket: NONE. Only "blue work shirt, jeans or khaki" (NPS).
3. Belt buckle: film-costume claim only, no cited photo/archive.
4. Hands (nails, ring, objects): NONE; smoking volume and "rolled smokes" are documented, brand (Chesterfield) is not.
5. Floors: Base Camp mess hall/barracks floors NONE; ranch house "wood" only from a family recollection of the pre-army house.
6. No source places Oppenheimer in a private room on the evening; documented places are mess hall, S-10000, outdoors in the rain (with Groves).
7. Hair: only "very closely clipped" at Los Alamos (Hempelmann); greying/nape/ears NONE.
8. Bird & Sherwin (American Prometheus) and the other memoirs (Rabi, Bethe, Kistiakowsky, Laura Fermi, McMillan, Wilson) could not be opened; all uses are via secondary pages. Elsie McMillan's lecture has no Oppenheimer appearance detail.
