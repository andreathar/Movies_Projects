# Director’s annotated script — reference edition v3

Draft companion to `01_script/script-en.md`. All spoken text below is unchanged from `dialogue-lines.csv`. All dialogue is fictional. Times are proposed allocations, not measured performances. This document does not approve the canonical screenplay.

## Dramatic spine

Andre begins by asking what it feels like to hear the future. Caesar asks about a name; the Khan about inheritance; Galileo about belief; Napoleon about laws and greatness; Oppenheimer about success and rightness. Each answer leaves a question that the next encounter cannot settle. The return to the present gives that responsibility to the audience.

## Timing and coverage

The picture remains 12 + (5 × 42) + 18 = 240 seconds across 41 planned slots. A speech is allowed to bridge cuts. The 10-second opening Andre shot is not a 10-second limit on the whole answer. Eye cues follow the approved recorded words; the current slot order is a planning grid. Move a reaction cut inside its scene when measured audio requires it. Do not speed up a performance to preserve this draft.

Opening voice references are separate test files. Their completed duration does not approve English pronunciation, naturalness, likeness or synchronization. Ending proposal: speech 0.5–14.2 seconds within the 18-second ending, leaving 3.8 seconds for the credit tail. The actual recording may require a shorter approved final sentence or revised credits timing.

## Julius Caesar — A name is not an answer

Does victory leave a republic, or personal power?

**Action:** The fingers press into the table, then stop. Andre lets the question land before answering.

**Light:** Warm, diffuse oil-lamp direction; steady exposure on skin. Ochre plaster and muted red.

**Sound:** Small flame, distant room tone, a dry sleeve against the grain. No cheering crowd.

**C-L01 — Caesar, offscreen** (1.0–3.5s proposed)
> Does my name survive?

Direction: Ask for permanence, with the confidence of someone used to yes.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**C-L02 — Andre** (4.3–13.1s proposed)
> It survives. People still study the victories and the reforms. They also study what one man's power did to a republic.

Direction: Recognition first, plainly; let 'a republic' carry the weight without pressing it.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

**C-L03 — Caesar, offscreen** (18.5–21.2s proposed)
> Then they remember a tyrant?

Direction: Test whether admiration has turned into condemnation.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**C-L04 — Andre** (22.2–28.2s proposed)
> They remember a question. Could the republic survive you? They are still asking it — about other men.

Direction: Give him back his own question. 'Other men' is said quietly — he hears he became a precedent.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

## Genghis Khan — What an empire carries

Can connection and destruction belong to the same legacy?

**Action:** One grip loosens. Andre stays upright and keeps a level, humane gaze.

**Light:** Felt-filtered amber and soft fire bounce. No moving flame shadow across eyes.

**Sound:** Wind in felt, restrained leather creak, low fire. Avoid battle spectacle.

**K-L01 — Khan, offscreen** (1.0–4.1s proposed)
> Will my sons keep what I have made?

Direction: Ask about what is left to the sons, not a list of victories.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**K-L02 — Andre** (4.9–14.1s proposed)
> They will make it larger than you can picture. Merchants, ideas, and knowledge will cross it. So will the news of cities that are no longer there.

Direction: Scale first, then the cost in one image. Do not swell on 'no longer there'.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

**K-L03 — Khan, offscreen** (18.5–20.9s proposed)
> And what will they call me?

Direction: Seek a name that can contain both outcomes.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**K-L04 — Andre** (21.9–28.6s proposed)
> Founder. Conqueror. Both words are still fighting over you. Neither one can make the dead speak.

Direction: Two words, flat. Hold the last clause; it is not a verdict.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

## Galileo Galilei — A question survives

Does silencing a person settle a question?

**Action:** A hand rests beside a blank page. Andre leans forward only a fraction, then becomes still.

**Light:** Soft cool window light, warm wood retained; page readable, face never overexposed.

**Sound:** Quiet air, a single paper movement, a faint chair creak. Let stillness carry the final beat.

**G-L01 — Galileo, offscreen** (1.0–3.5s proposed)
> Am I finally believed?

Direction: Ask with fatigue and a remaining thread of hope.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**G-L02 — Andre** (4.3–13.7s proposed)

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

## Napoleon Bonaparte — The price of greatness

Can greatness be separated from its price?

**Action:** A hand stops above a closed book. Andre does not lean away from the second question.

**Light:** Muted sea-grey window light and damp plaster. Preserve skin warmth without a sepia wash.

**Sound:** Distant wind, restrained room creak, cloth. Sea stays outside the scene.

**N-L01 — Napoleon, offscreen** (1.0–3.8s proposed)
> Does France keep my laws?

Direction: Look for an institution that survives exile.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**N-L02 — Andre** (4.6–13.1s proposed)
> Your code outlives your throne. Other nations borrow from it. It promises equality — and still finds people to leave out.

Direction: Equality and exclusion in one breath; no scolding.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

## J. Robert Oppenheimer — What comes next

Can technical success answer a moral question?

**Action:** A resting hand loses its tension. Andre stops speaking and stays present for the silence.

**Light:** Dusty pre-dawn blue and one muted practical lamp. No flash, mushroom cloud or reflected face.

**Sound:** Sparse desert wind and room tone. No dramatic detonation under the city names.

**O-L01 — Oppenheimer, offscreen** (1.0–3.3s proposed)
> Does the test work?

Direction: Ask the narrow technical question first.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**O-L02 — Andre** (4.1–15.9s proposed)
> It works. Three weeks later, Hiroshima. Three days after that, Nagasaki. The war ends that summer. What it cost — and whether it had to — people are still asking.

Direction: Facts, evenly paced; give the two city names room; do not perform grief.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

**O-L03 — Oppenheimer, offscreen** (18.5–21.4s proposed)
> Can you tell me whether it was right?

Direction: Drop the scientific certainty. Ask a human question.

Coverage: Offscreen throughout

**O-L04 — Andre** (22.4–25.8s proposed)
> I can tell you what came next. I can't tell you that.

Direction: The limit of knowing. 'I can't tell you that' is quiet and final; at least 4 s silence follows.

Coverage: Visible mouth requires approved speech route; dialogue may bridge reviewed inserts.

**Design review:** The eye pair changes brow darkness and gaze. The meeting is fictional. No weapons diagrams, unsupported casualty totals or claimed quotation from the test.

## Ending

**OUT-L01 — Andre** (0.8–10.5s proposed)
> I went looking for answers. I came back with their question. One day someone will sit across from us, knowing what came next. What will they have to tell us?

Direction: Address one person. 'Their question' is Oppenheimer's; the last sentence is ours.

Coverage: Carry the final clause over the empty chair/table; end speech before the credit tail.
